\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Multimodal HSI-LiDAR Classification}

Recent HSI-LiDAR fusion backbones can be viewed along the stage at which spectral and spatial information are coupled, ranging from early fusion to feature-level interaction and decision-level integration~\cite{mohla2020fusatnet,fang2022s2enet,hang2020coupled,li2022mahidfnet,ghosh2023hct}.
Representative designs include FusAtNet's cross-modality attention~\cite{mohla2020fusatnet}, S2ENet's spectral--spatial enhancement~\cite{fang2022s2enet}, Coupled CNNs with partially shared streams~\cite{hang2020coupled}, MAHiDFNet's multi-attention decision fusion~\cite{li2022mahidfnet}, and HCT's hierarchical CNN-Transformer fusion with cross-token attention~\cite{ghosh2023hct}.
Recent variants further explore masked self-attention fusion~\cite{shi2025msafn}.
While these methods improve fixed-label HSI-LiDAR classification, they are designed for static training protocols and do not address how branch-level spectral--spatial representations drift under simultaneous class growth and cross-domain distribution shift.

\subsection{Remote-Sensing Class-Incremental Learning}

Remote-sensing CIL has begun to adapt generic continual-learning strategies to hyperspectral and multimodal classification.
For single-modality HSI CIL, DCPRN combines distillation with prototype representation learning~\cite{deng2024dcprn}, while CrossACL and JFI-ACL apply analytic continual learning~\cite{xu2025crossacl,zhang2024jfi_acl}.
Recent extensions further explore feature-space expansion with spatial--spectral augmentation~\cite{feica2025} and few-shot HSI CIL through Mamba-based precognition--retrospection~\cite{hyperpret2026}.
For multimodal remote sensing, PMPT applies prototype-based meta-prompt tuning over a pre-trained backbone for few-shot incremental classification~\cite{yang2026pmpt}, and DF-PEM uses dual-flow prompt experts with Mamba for incremental multimodal classification~\cite{li2026dfpem}.
These methods are predominantly evaluated within a single scene, sensor, or dataset, leaving fine-grained multimodal HSI-LiDAR CDCIL less studied: class growth occurs within each domain, while cross-sensor distribution shift occurs across domains.

\subsection{Cross-Domain Class-Incremental Learning}

Cross-domain class-incremental learning in remote sensing has begun to address concurrent class growth and domain shift, but existing settings differ from multimodal pixel-level HSI-LiDAR CDCIL in modality, task granularity, or training regime.
HyperKD~\cite{li2025hyperkd} performs lifelong hyperspectral classification through exemplar replay and knowledge distillation, but treats each entire dataset as a single coarse task and operates on HSI alone.
CDCIRSC~\cite{zhang2024cdcirssc} addresses cross-domain CIL for remote-sensing scene classification, and CDIL~\cite{tian2026cdil} extends this direction through domain-specific prototypes and contrastive learning, but they target image- or scene-level classification rather than pixel-level multimodal land-cover.
Related cross-dataset remote-sensing transfer methods, such as multimodal prompt tuning~\cite{liu2025prompt} and cross-scene prototype alignment~\cite{xie2024prototype}, adapt across datasets through joint or aligned training, but do not address incremental class growth.
Our setting therefore couples multimodal HSI-LiDAR fusion, fine-grained per-domain class-incremental tasks, and cross-sensor distribution shift over multiple sensors and scenes.

\subsection{Feature-Space Class-Incremental Learning}

Feature-space CIL methods mitigate forgetting by operating on extracted representations, class prototypes, or feature-space corrections instead of repeatedly updating the full backbone.
Representative frozen-feature methods include FeTrIL's pseudo-feature translation~\cite{petit2023fetril}, RanPAC's random projection over pre-trained features~\cite{mcdonnell2023ranpac}, SimpleCIL's prototype classifier~\cite{zhou2025simplecil}, and FeCAM's class-covariance modeling~\cite{goswami2023fecam}.
A related line addresses feature drift or PTM-based adaptation through learnable drift compensation~\cite{gomezvilla2024ldc}, classifier alignment~\cite{gao2023slca}, expandable subspace ensembles~\cite{zhou2024ease}, low-rank random features~\cite{liang2025loranpac}, and knowledge memorization mechanisms~\cite{gao2025kmr}.
Our method is related to this feature-space paradigm but differs in two respects: the backbone is trained from scratch during first-domain warm-up and then frozen, rather than initialized from external pre-training; and the spectral branch serves as a parameter-free router that selects among per-domain classifier heads updated incrementally as new classes arrive.
Thus, the open question is not whether feature-space CIL can work with strong pre-trained visual backbones, but how to construct stable and discriminative feature spaces for multimodal HSI-LiDAR streams under cross-sensor domain shift.
